A Rothemund rectangle: the most reproduced structure in the field, folded and imaged. It gates everything else. If it doesn’t work here, the problem is in our chain, and every later result would inherit it.

Flatness is the one we don’t know

The square lattice implies 10.67 bp/turn against DNA’s relaxed 10.5, which would twist the sheet. We cancel it by deleting one base every 64 — exact on paper. Whether it cancels in practice is visible in an AFM image and nowhere else.

E5 failing is a real result about origami manufacturing. E6 failing is a real result about molecular coupling, and is publishable.

E7 is off the critical path entirely: different instrument, no dependency on the hinge line.

At the bench

Measure the tip. Before and after.

A half-height footprint is wider than the object by twice √(R·h − h²/4). For a 10 nm tip on a 2 nm plate that is 8.7 nm — a quarter of a 36 nm width.

A nominal “10 nm” tip is routinely 8–20 nm in use, and the correction goes as √R. The tooling refuses to run without a measured value rather than substituting the nominal one.

How a run works

  1. pre/ — plan, predictions, protocol, oligo order. Write the gate, then commit it before the session.
  2. during/ — session log, instrument settings, raw files untouched.
  3. post/ — produced by the toolchain. Nothing here is edited by hand.
Why the commit matters

The comparison will happily reconcile a measurement with whatever is predicted. A prediction adjusted after seeing a scan produces output identical to one made in advance — nothing in the numbers distinguishes them, so the distinction lives in the record. The tooling checks the timestamps and says so.

dobladillo afm read     during/raw/scan.gsf
dobladillo experiment report  <folder>

Send scans as Gwyddion Simple Field (.gsf): open in Gwyddion, pick the height channel, Save As. One export covers every instrument.

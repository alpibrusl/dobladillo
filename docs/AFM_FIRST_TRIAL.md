# First AFM trial: the rectangle

An experiment design to hand to a group with an AFM. One sample, one session.

Scope is deliberate: **only the first closed loop**, not the roadmap. Design a
rectangle with the toolchain, fold it, image it, compare. The ask is small
because the rectangle is the field's standard calibration sample, so imaging
one is close to routine for any origami-capable setup.

A formatted version for sharing is published separately; this is the source of
record, and every number in it is generated by the toolchain rather than typed.

## The sample

| Property | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Scaffold | M13mp18, 7249 nt | 6930 nt routed; 319 nt unpaired loop |
| Staples | 220, 30-32 nt | standard desalted, 25 nmol scale, ~EUR 693 |
| Lattice | square, 22 helices x 320 bp | twist-corrected, one base deleted every 64 |
| Predicted footprint | 106.2 x 66.0 nm | from design geometry, not simulation |
| Expected height | ~2 nm | one helix layer, dried |

**Stated plainly in the document:** this is Rothemund-*style*, not the
published 2006 staple set. Routing and every staple come from our toolchain,
which is the thing being tested. It should fold; it never has.

## Preparation, in five steps

1. **Stocks.** 10x folding buffer without magnesium (5 mM Tris, 1 mM EDTA, pH
   8.0); 1 M MgCl2 added per reaction; scaffold quantified by A260; staples
   pooled equimolar from three plates.
2. **Folding as a magnesium series.** Nine 50 uL reactions at 5, 7, 9, 11, 13,
   15, 17, 19, 20 mM MgCl2. Scaffold 10 nM, staples 50 nM each. Hold 65 C for
   15 min, ramp 60 -> 40 C at 1 C/min: about 35 minutes, because this is 2D.
3. **Gel check.** 2% agarose, 0.5x TBE with 11 mM MgCl2, cold, low voltage,
   against bare scaffold. Picks the one condition that travels.
4. **Purification.** PEG precipitation into folding buffer at the same
   magnesium; 100 kDa spin filters as fallback.
5. **Deposition on mica.** Cleave fresh; dilute to 1-2 nM in 10 mM MgCl2;
   10 uL droplet, 1-5 min; rinse ~200 uL ultrapure water; dry under nitrogen
   2-3 min.

Step 5 is written from the literature, not from experience, and the document
says so and asks to be corrected. It also offers liquid imaging as preferred if
their setup makes it easy, since drying flattens.

## The imaging ask

- 2 x 2 um for counting and classifying (~4 nm/px at 512): well-formed vs
  malformed vs aggregate.
- 500 x 500 nm for dimensions (~1 nm/px), well inside the 10% agreement target.
- **300 well-separated particles if cheap, 100 as the floor.** The precision of
  a fraction depends only on the count: at 100 particles a yield near 70%
  carries a 95% interval of about +-9%, at 300 about +-5%. Below 100 it stops
  distinguishing a good fold from a mediocre one, which is the distinction the
  trial exists for. Dimensions need far fewer; 50 clean particles give a mean.

## What it tests

Long edge, short edge, height and well-formed fraction all have predicted
counterparts already computed. The one we are least sure of is flatness: the
square lattice implies 10.67 bp/turn against a relaxed 10.5 and would twist the
sheet; we cancel it by deleting one base every 64, which works exactly on
paper. **Whether it cancels in practice is visible in an AFM image and nowhere
else.**

## What it asks of them

Four questions, all of the form "tell us we are wrong": is the deposition
sensible, fold there or bring it folded, purified or crude, and is any of this
simply naive. An honest read is more useful than a successful image.

## What comes back

Native instrument files rather than exported images, with the acquisition
settings, so item M4's pipeline can read them. Measurements go to the registry
with their conditions attached. A disagreement between prediction and
measurement is the result, not a failure to tidy away.
